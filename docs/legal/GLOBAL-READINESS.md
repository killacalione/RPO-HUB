# Worldwide readiness: scoped legal examples and launch gaps

Owner requirement: worldwide availability from the first public release, children/teens/adults, public same-age child communication, scripted worlds and text/proximity/party voice. **This research does not establish worldwide legal clearance.** It illustrates US/EU/UK/Australia; remaining countries, subnational laws, sanctions/provider restrictions, representation, content, privacy, recording, taxes and payment rules remain unassessed. No policies/contracts/registrations reviewed by counsel or executed.

Worldwide availability does not imply identical account eligibility and permissions everywhere. A country-aware policy may be needed for age/guardian rules, permitted communication, content and data handling. Record the actual operator/entity, targeted users and countries; do not use a worldwide checkbox or gaming label as legal classification. Features cannot be promised in regions where applicable obligations remain unmet.

| Region | Current primary framework / source type | Discovery consequence |
|---|---|---|
| US | COPPA binding rule; FTC implementation guidance [S30, S31] | Covered under 13 collection/disclosure needs notice/appropriate consent and parent/security/retention controls; same-age rooms are not an exemption |
| EU | GDPR binding regulation; Commission territorial/child-consent explanations [S71, S72] | Free services targeting/monitoring EU users can be covered. When relying on consent for directly offered online services, national parent-consent thresholds 13–16 vary; these are not universal minimum account ages. Purposes/lawful bases/transfers/rights/representation need review |
| EU platforms | DSA binding law; voluntary minors guidance [S73, S79] | Classification/size matter. Qualifying micro/small Section 3 exception includes Article 28; it does not exempt GDPR or all DSA intermediary/hosting duties |
| UK | Statutory Children's Code explanation and Ofcom Online Safety guidance [S74, S75] | Assess covered under 18 access, risk/privacy/contact/reporting and overseas UK links. User-to-user gaming is not a blanket safety exemption. Current child safety duties already operate |
| Australia | Age-restricted social-media rule/exclusion rules and regulator guidance [S76, S77] | Covered services must take reasonable steps preventing under 16 accounts from December 10 2025. Sole/primary gaming-purpose exclusions depend on actual functionality/use; this broad social/creator hub is not assumed excluded |
| Rest of world | **Unknown / not reviewed** | Country-specific review and operating eligibility remain required work; no universal launch conclusion |

## Future UK watch item

Official June 15 2026 announcement proposes under 16 social-media restrictions and stranger-communication changes, including gaming, with first protections possibly spring 2027 [S78]. It is a future policy/implementation announcement, not proof of today's operative blanket ban or final scope. Same-age public participants can still be strangers. Recheck enacted regulations and dates before a release that overlaps them.

## Age assurance and data minimization

EDPB interpretive guidance promotes necessary/proportionate assurance, not indiscriminate identity collection [S70]. Define exact age bands, minimum supported age, country eligibility, assurance, parent choices and contact scopes separately. User-entered age alone cannot guarantee real-user cohort membership; collecting everyone's ID is not presumed required. Processor/hosting/auth/media logs and uploaded content need actual data maps. Public world listing does not require public child personal information.

## Operating gate and registry

Create a country register with status, operator/service classification, account ages, consent basis/process, content/contact rules, data/transfer/representation questions, source/date, responsible reviewer and next milestone. This paper has not completed that register for every country. Do not label an unreviewed region ready.

Owner limited maintainer capacity is effort, not a response schedule. Assign a primary/backup responder, monitored alerts, removal authority and escalation for urgent child/content incidents and applicable deadlines. TIDA's valid-request 48-hour deadline for covered platforms includes weekends [S32]. Automation can route/quarantine/report; it does not establish correct decisions or staffed response. Moderation language coverage, complaints/appeals and privacy rights remain unfunded/unverified responsibilities.

A capacity-limited global beta with staffed communication sessions is a **proposal** to preserve global access while limiting admitted rooms/users/time. Actual countries/features remain subject to applicable review; it does not complete the worldwide requirement if broad regions are still ineligible. Stable public discovery/creation can be considered separately from live communication eligibility, with data/content scope still reviewed. No publication or account data collection authorized from this backlog alone.

## Round-six child privacy settings

Owner-selected space visibility is confirmed; available settings do not remove country/guardian/contact duties. ICO standard 7 calls for high-privacy defaults in covered UK child-facing services unless a compelling child-best-interests reason justifies a different default [S85]. Proposed Private initial selection is one part of the design, not complete compliance or a universal identical legal rule. Preserve reviewed choices; changes need understandable explanations and risk review.
